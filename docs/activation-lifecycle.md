# Activation Lifecycle

A brand activation — an event, a sampling, a launch, a partnership night — is
a project with a hard deadline and no second take. It gets the same
discipline as a build: decide what success means before you start, and
measure it after you finish.

---

## The Five Stages

| Stage | Build equivalent | Output | Template |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Brief** | Discuss | Goal, audience, KPIs, budget, constraints | [`ACTIVATION_BRIEF`](../templates/ACTIVATION_BRIEF.template.md) |
| **2. Plan** | Plan | Timeline, partners, people, stock, risks | the brief's plan section |
| **3. Run** | Execute | The day, run to a sheet | [`RUN_SHEET`](../templates/RUN_SHEET.template.md) |
| **4. Report** | Review | Actuals against the KPIs set in the brief | [`EVENT_REPORT`](../templates/EVENT_REPORT.template.md) |
| **5. Learn** | Verify | What changes next time, filed where next year's plan will find it | the report's learnings section |

## Stage Rules

**Brief — set the numbers before you start.**
KPIs written after the event are a story, not a measurement. Every brief
names the goal, the audience, two to four KPIs with targets, and the budget.
If a KPI cannot be measured on the day, change it or plan how to measure it.

**Plan — work back from the date.**
List every dependency with an owner and a date: venue, partners, staff,
stock, permits, content, logistics. The riskiest dependency gets the earliest
date.

**Check the rules for your category.**
Some product categories are restricted in how they can be marketed — what
can be shown, said, offered or posted, and to whom. Check local rules and
your company's policy at the brief stage, not the week before.

**Run — one sheet, everyone has it.**
The run sheet is the single source of truth on the day: times, people, stock,
setup and pack-down, contacts, and the plan B for the two most likely
failures.

**Report — within two working days.**
Memory fades and receipts get lost. Report actuals against each target, the
cost per contact, and three lines on what worked and what didn't. Send it to
whoever approved the budget.

**Learn — file it where next year's plan will look.**
The learnings go into the annual calendar entry for that activation, so the
next plan for the same date starts from what you found, not from scratch.

## The Annual Calendar

Most activation years repeat: the same seasons, the same festivals, the same
holiday peaks. Keep one calendar of the fixed points, and attach each year's
report to its date. After one year you plan from data; after two you can see
trends.
